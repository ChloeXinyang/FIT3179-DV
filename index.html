FIT3179 Data Visualisation 2 · Australian Census: People and Communities

One Country,
Many Communities

How age, education, work, income and household structure differ across Australia.

National averages can make Australia look uniform. The 2021 Census tells a more varied story: communities differ sharply by age, qualifications, work, household income and household structure. This page follows those differences from the national picture down to Statistical Area Level 2 (SA2), then finishes with a more recent June 2026 administrative snapshot.

01 · Population & age

A country of different ages

Population is unevenly distributed, and the age profile of a community depends strongly on where it is.

Population is concentrated in three large states

Tile area represents population. NSW, Victoria and Queensland together account for 77.6% of residents across the eight main states and territories.

Source: Australian Bureau of Statistics, 2021 Census General Community Profile (G01), place of usual residence.

Where are Australia's youngest and oldest communities?

Median age is mapped by SA2. Lighter areas are younger; darker areas are older. Hover for local values.

Grey areas have no valid resident median-age value.

Source: ABS 2021 Census General Community Profile (G02); ASGS Edition 3 (2021) SA2 geography.

Australia’s population bulges through the working-age years

The largest five-year age group in 2021 was 30–34. At older ages, women increasingly outnumber men.

Male values are mirrored only to create the population-pyramid layout; tooltip values remain positive counts.

Source: ABS 2021 Census General Community Profile (G04), place of usual residence.

Age profiles differ across states and territories

Each cell is a share of that jurisdiction’s population, allowing fair comparison despite large differences in total population.

The Northern Territory and ACT have especially large 25–44 shares, while Tasmania and South Australia have the highest 65+ shares.

Source: ABS 2021 Census General Community Profile.

02 · Education & work

Education and work change through life

Qualification profiles differ across generations, while participation in work changes sharply from youth through retirement ages.

Qualification profiles shift noticeably across age groups

Bachelor degrees are most prominent among 25–44-year-olds, while Certificate III/IV takes a larger share in several younger and older working-age groups.

Percentages use the age-group totals in Census table G49 for highest non-school qualification. “Other / not stated” is retained because it becomes substantial at older ages.

Source: ABS 2021 Census General Community Profile, Table G49.

Work participation changes strongly across the life course

Full-time employment accounts for about half of people aged 25–54 in this table, while being outside the labour force dominates after age 65.

“Unemployed” and “Not in the labour force” are different Census categories. Bars are normalised to 100% within each age group.

Source: ABS 2021 Census General Community Profile, Table G46.

03 · Education & income

Education and income vary across place

The next three views move from geography to relationship and then to within-state distributions.

Education and household income form distinct geographic patterns

Each SA2 is placed into low, middle or high thirds on both Bachelor+ attainment and median weekly household income.

Degree-qualified communities tend to have higher household incomes

Each point is one SA2 with at least 500 residents. The overall relationship is positive, but community-level association does not establish individual-level causation.

The black line is a simple overall linear trend. Small-population SA2s are excluded to reduce instability.

Source: ABS 2021 Census General Community Profile, Tables G02 and G43.

Income varies within states as well as between them

Each ridge summarises the distribution of SA2 median weekly household incomes inside one jurisdiction.

Each SA2 contributes one observation. These are distributions of community median incomes, not the income distribution of individual residents.

Source: ABS 2021 Census General Community Profile, Table G02.

04 · Households

Life inside Australian households

Household size and household type are closely related, and family and non-family households occupy different parts of the income distribution.

Household size changes the balance between family and non-family living

Column width shows how common each household size is; vertical height shows the family/non-family split within that size.

One- and two-person households together account for about 59.1% of households. Among two-person households, about 91.8% are family households.

Source: ABS 2021 Census General Community Profile, Table G35.

Family and non-family households occupy very different income ranges

The two profiles are normalised separately, so the comparison is about income composition rather than absolute household counts.

“Income partly/not stated” is excluded from plotted percentages.

Source: ABS 2021 Census General Community Profile, Table G33.

05 · Recent snapshot

A more recent administrative view

The Census provides the detailed community profile used above. DSS administrative data add a June 2026 view of JobSeeker Payment recipient counts.

Where are larger JobSeeker recipient counts recorded?

Circle area represents recipient count in each SA2. The map is intentionally a count view, not a population-adjusted rate.

Symbols use SA2 representative points, not exact recipient locations. DSS JobSeeker counts are not the same concept as the 2021 Census unemployment rate.

Source: Australian Government Department of Social Services, Benefit and Payment Recipient Demographics, June 2026; ABS ASGS Edition 3 (2021) geography.

There is no single “average” Australian community

Age, qualifications, work, household income and household structure combine differently across places and life stages. Breaking national averages down by geography reveals patterns that disappear in a single national number. The 2021 Census remains the detailed foundation for this comparison, while the 2026 DSS snapshot provides a later, differently defined administrative perspective.
